01 — Theory

Understanding the Physics

A Faraday cage is a mesh of conducting material that blocks electromagnetic fields. Here's the science behind why it works.

01
🔲

What Is a Faraday Cage?

An enclosure made of conductive material — wire mesh, sheet metal, or foil — that blocks external electric fields. Invented by Michael Faraday in 1836. The conductors allow free electrons to move and rearrange instantly in response to any applied field.

Named after Michael Faraday (1836)
02
⚡

Electric Fields at Conductors

In static equilibrium, the electric field inside a conductor is exactly zero. Any external field causes free electrons to rearrange until the internal field they create perfectly cancels the external one. The net field at every interior point becomes zero.

E_inside = 0 (at equilibrium)
03
🌀

Charge Redistribution

When an electric field approaches, free electrons in the metal move toward the positive end of the external field, leaving a positive "shadow" on the opposite side. This induced surface charge distribution creates an equal and opposite field inside — complete cancellation.

σ_induced → −E_external / ε₀
04
📐

Gauss's Law Explanation

Gauss's law states that the electric flux through any closed surface equals the enclosed charge divided by ε₀. Since the conductor encloses no free charges, the total flux — and hence the electric field — through any Gaussian surface inside the cage is zero.

∮ E⃗ · dA⃗ = Q_enc / ε₀ = 0
05
🌊

Maxwell's Equations (Conceptual)

Maxwell's equations describe how electric and magnetic fields propagate and interact with matter. For a perfect conductor, the boundary conditions force the tangential E-field to zero at the surface, and the field cannot penetrate the interior — this is the physical foundation of shielding.

∇ × E = −∂B/∂t ; ∇ · D = ρ
06
📡

Mesh Size & Frequency

A solid cage is ideal, but a mesh works too — as long as the hole size is much smaller than the wavelength of incoming EM radiation. A 2.4 GHz WiFi signal has a ~12 cm wavelength; a mesh with <1 cm holes will block it effectively. Higher frequency → smaller holes needed.

Hole size ≪ λ = c / f
02 — Step-by-Step

How Shielding Happens

Follow the chain of events when an electric field meets a conducting cage.

STEP 01

Electromagnetic Wave Approaches

An oscillating electric field (shown as colored field lines) propagates toward the metallic cage. At this stage, the field is at full strength and travels at the speed of light in the medium.

STEP 02

Free Electrons Respond Instantly

The moment the field reaches the conductor surface, free conduction electrons experience a force (F = qE) and begin moving — essentially at the speed of light propagation through the metal. This happens on the timescale of femtoseconds.

STEP 03

Surface Charge Accumulates

Electrons pile up on the side facing the field (making it negative), leaving positive ions exposed on the opposite side. This is called induced polarization — the cage itself becomes a dipole.

STEP 04

Internal Field Cancels to Zero

The induced charge distribution creates its own electric field inside the cage that exactly opposes the external field. The vector sum is zero everywhere inside. Objects inside the cage experience no electric force — complete shielding achieved.

STEP 05

Dynamic Equilibrium for AC Fields

For oscillating fields (AC or EM waves), this redistribution happens continuously, every half-cycle. The electrons oscillate back and forth, always maintaining cancellation inside. The cage re-radiates some of the energy — this is why it also blocks WiFi and radio signals.

05 — Real World

Where Faraday Cages Are Used

From protecting aircraft to keeping your microwave from irradiating the kitchen — Faraday shielding is everywhere.

⚡

Lightning Protection

Car bodies and aircraft fuselages act as cages — lightning current flows around the occupants, not through them.

📡

Microwave Ovens

The metal mesh on the door blocks 2.45 GHz microwaves from escaping — the holes are far smaller than the wavelength.

✈️

Aircraft Shielding

Aluminum fuselages protect avionics and passengers from lightning strikes and external EM interference.

🏥

MRI Rooms

Hospital MRI suites are fully shielded rooms preventing external RF interference from corrupting sensitive scans.

💳

RFID Wallets

Metal-lined wallets block wireless skimming of contactless payment cards by attenuating the 13.56 MHz NFC field.

🔬

Lab Equipment

Sensitive electronics like oscilloscopes and spectrum analyzers are housed in grounded metal cases to eliminate noise.

🚗

Car Electronics

ECU and control modules are shielded within grounded enclosures protecting them from the engine's EM emissions.

🔐

TEMPEST / SCIF

Secure compartmented intelligence facilities use full-room Faraday shielding to prevent electronic eavesdropping.
